Go 10–24 September for the warmest sea and settled weather, or early-to-mid October if price matters more. October drops the hotel rate noticeably and the island goes quiet again, at the cost of higher rain risk and shorter days.

Avoid late October onwards. The magic doesn't survive the storms.

Getting there and getting around

Flights

No direct route from Berlin. TAP via Lisbon is cleanest: roughly 3 h 15 to Lisbon, 2 h 20 onward, 7–8 hours door to door. Scheduled Airbus A320 and A321neo jets, not charters. Return fares run €280–450.

Ignore the nine-hour "average" figures on comparison sites. Those include miserable layovers.

On the island

You won't drive, and you don't need to. Every tour listed above includes hotel pickup, which is the single strongest argument for staying central.

Public buses exist but serve locals, not viewpoints. They will not get you to Sete Cidades or Lagoa do Fogo. Budget about €85 for airport transfers and occasional taxis.

Where to stay

Find Portas da Cidade on the map and book within 600 metres of it. That single rule covers everything: tour pickups, dinner, the market, and the marina your boat leaves from.

The zone

  • South edge — Avenida Infante Dom Henrique, the seafront road
  • East edge — Portas do Mar marina
  • West edge — Forte de São Brás and Campo de São Francisco
  • North edge — Mercado da Graça, five minutes uphill

Worth shortlisting

  • Hotel Talisman — inside the pedestrian zone, rooftop pool over the harbour. Best location of the four.
  • NINE DOTS — highest rated, small and quiet. Ask for a room away from the pizza oven.
  • Hotel Gaivota — seafront, closest to the marina, breakfast included. Best value.
  • Vila Galé Collection — converted historic building, two pools, central but quiet.

The Booking.com trap. Searching "Ponta Delgada" returns places that aren't in it — Lagoa, Ribeira Grande, Capelas, Vila Franca, Mosteiros, and rural houses 40–60 minutes away that need a car to reach. They look like bargains and are unusable.

Switch to map view immediately and pick only from pins beside Portas da Cidade. Ignore the list view entirely.

Two to think twice about: NEAT Hotel Avenida and Hotel Camões. Central and cheap, but both draw repeated complaints about thin walls.

Filters that matter more than price

  • Breakfast included — you're leaving at 08:00 on tour days
  • Free cancellation — weather-volatile destination
  • Walking distance to Portas do Mar — check it before confirming

Is it safe

Yes, comfortably. Portugal sits in the global top ten for peacefulness and the Azores are calmer than the mainland. Safer than Berlin by a wide margin.

Wildlife

No snakes. No scorpions. Not rare — none. The islands rose from the ocean and were never attached to a continent, so nothing crawled here. The only native land mammal is a bat.

Minor sea business only: sea urchins on rocky shores, and the occasional Portuguese man o' war washed up. Water shoes on rocky entries, don't touch blue things on the sand.

Earthquakes

The only item with real substance. Three tectonic plates meet here and small tremors are routine — but São Miguel hasn't had a land eruption since 1630. The steaming ground at Furnas is the attraction, not the threat.

CIVISA runs permanent seismic monitoring with the University of the Azores and publishes alerts. You are not walking into an unmonitored situation.

Health

Trivially easy. Your EHIC works directly. Ponta Delgada has a full hospital. Tap water is drinkable, no vaccinations needed, no tropical disease.

Pirates

Historically, genuinely yes. The islands were the last waypoint for treasure fleets returning from the Americas, which drew English privateers, French corsairs and Barbary raiders through the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. That's why the coast is studded with forts.

Today, none. Walk through Forte de São Brás on the waterfront to see the evidence.

The risks that are actually real:

Fog and wind disrupting flights. It happens several times a year. Don't book anything tight on your return day.

Wet trails and unfenced viewpoints. Volcanic rock plus constant moisture. Most tourist injuries here are falls.

Seasickness on the whale boat. Atlantic swell, small boats. Take a tablet an hour before.

Sunburn through cloud. UV is stronger than the grey sky suggests.

The window is 22 September to 12 October. Three things have to overlap and they only do once a year: the Train de Charlevoix runs June to October, whale season peaks August to October, and foliage lands late September to mid-October.

Tadoussac is a seasonal village. Much of it closes after mid-October.

The detail that should change your route

Foliage peaks north to south — Charlevoix and Saguenay in the last week of September, Québec City in early-to-mid October, Montréal mid-to-late October. The Normal itinerary travels northeast, which runs against the colour, so you'd reach Charlevoix after its peak.

Fix it by arriving around 25 September, which threads Charlevoix correctly in the first week of October. Montréal being pre-peak doesn't matter; it isn't a foliage destination.

Avoid 9–11 October. Canadian Thanksgiving falls on Monday 11 October 2027 — the biggest domestic travel weekend of the autumn.

Charlevoix and Tadoussac are exactly where Québécois go for it. Small inventory, fills completely, prices jump. Either be in a city that weekend or book the rural legs many months ahead.

Getting there and getting around

Flights

Usually one stop to Montréal, around €450–750 in October shoulder season. Fly into YUL; it has the most options and the cheapest fares.

Entry

As a German citizen you need an eTA before boarding — CAD 7, applied online, usually approved in minutes, valid five years. Don't leave it to the airport.

Trains and coaches

  • VIA Rail Corridor — Montréal to Québec City 3 h 15, Montréal to Ottawa 2 h, Toronto to Montréal 5 h
  • Train de Charlevoix — Québec City area to Baie-Saint-Paul and La Malbaie, June to October only, 125 km of shoreline
  • Intercar coach — La Malbaie or Québec City to Tadoussac
  • Bus 800 — downtown Québec to Montmorency Falls, about CAD 3.50, 35–40 min
  • Funicular — upper to lower town, CAD 3.50
  • Lévis ferry — a few dollars, ten minutes, the best skyline view there is

One fare caution. The Train de Charlevoix changed operator in 2026 and travellers report one-way fares are no longer clearly published, which pushes people into pricier returns. Check before you commit.

Two traps that catch Europeans.

Tax is added at the register, never shown on the price. Québec stacks GST 5 % plus QST 9.975 % plus a 3.5 % lodging tax — about 18.5 % on a room. Ontario is 13 % HST.

Tipping 15–20 % is expected, not optional. Together these turn a CAD 25 menu item into roughly CAD 34. Budget for it or you'll feel ambushed daily.

Two levers if you want it cheaper

  • Auberges in the small towns. Baie-Saint-Paul and Tadoussac both have good ones at CAD 110–140 rather than hotel rates.
  • Apportez votre vin. Montréal restaurants where you bring your own bottle. Takes a real bite out of dinner.

Why no Toronto

Good transit, very safe, genuinely great food diversity — and not magical. It's a competent large North American city and nothing in it will give you the feeling you're chasing. Its one real asset is access to Niagara Falls, and Montmorency already covers "dramatic waterfall".

Going northeast instead of southwest buys you Charlevoix and Tadoussac: the part of Québec that earns the transatlantic flight, and among the very few pieces of Canadian wilderness reachable without driving.

If you'd rather have winter

Carnaval de Québec and the Hôtel de Glace run late January to early March. Spectacular, and closer in spirit to what Iceland gave you — but understand it's a different trip, not a different date. No Charlevoix train, no whales, no Tadoussac. Montréal and Québec City only, about 8–9 days rather than 14, at −15 °C to −25 °C.

That's continental cold, far harsher than Iceland's maritime cold. Different category of commitment.

The Jeonbuk World Seoye Biennale runs in odd-numbered years, in autumn. The organiser's own record is consistent:

Also in October

Hangeul Day, 9 October — a national holiday for the alphabet itself, with exhibitions around it. If your dates include it, be in Seoul.

Getting there and getting around

Flights

No direct route from Berlin. One stop via Istanbul, Frankfurt, Munich, Helsinki, Zurich or Doha. Budget €600–850 return in October, 13–15 hours door to door.

Entry

The K-ETA exemption for German citizens ends 31 December 2026. From 1 January 2027 it is mandatory again for all visa-free nationalities. ₩10,000, roughly €6.50, at k-eta.go.kr, approved before boarding.

Another extension is plausible — it's been extended before — and any announcement would come around November 2026. Assume you need it.

The calligraphy circuit

Seoul

  • Seoul Calligraphy Art Museum — at Seoul Arts Center, Seocho. Korea's dedicated seoye museum
  • Insadong — the working end. Brushes, ink stones, hanji, seal carving. Get a 도장 made; it takes a day
  • National Hangeul Museum — Yongsan. Check its current operating status before building a day around it
  • Jongmyo Shrine and Changdeokgung for the Joseon context

Beyond Seoul

  • Jeonju — the Biennale, and the hanji mills. Korea's paper is made here
  • Haeinsa — the Tripitaka Koreana. 80,000 woodblocks carved 1237–1248, in a hall whose ventilation engineering has preserved them for eight centuries. Probably the most meaningful object in the country for someone whose working life is typography
  • Andong — Dosan and Byeongsan Seowon, the Confucian academies
